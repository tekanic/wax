Where Wax is ahead

  • Speed

    Wax is drawn on the GPU with GPUI, the framework behind the Zed editor, and every header is cached on your Mac in SQLite. Folders open the moment you click, search covers mail you haven't opened, and nothing waits on a web page to render. Spark, by contrast, ships a whole Chromium browser inside the app: 373 MB of its 720 MB.

  • Security

    Wax is written in Rust, which rules out a whole class of bugs before the app is built. That class matters: Google found that about 70% of serious Chrome security bugs were memory-safety problems, and Microsoft reported the same share for its own security fixes. HTML mail opens in a sandboxed WebKit view with scripts off and remote images blocked until you ask for them.

  • Plugins that can't phone home

    Thunderbird add-ons are JavaScript that asks you for permissions. Apple's MailKit is narrow enough that many Apple Mail plug-ins never came back after Apple dropped the old kind in macOS Sonoma. Wax plugins run in WebAssembly with no files, no network and limits on memory and time, so a plugin can sort, label and warn but can't send your mail anywhere.

  • Your login stays yours

    There's no Wax account and no Wax server. Your Google sign-in lives in the macOS Keychain and Wax connects to Gmail itself. Spark keeps account credentials, encrypted, on its own servers so it can run features like Send Later, and the new Outlook for Mac connects through Microsoft's cloud.

  • Open source, at no cost

    Only Wax and Thunderbird let you read every line. Wax is free under the GPL-3.0, and the plugin interface is MIT, so anyone can write and share plugins. Compare Mimestream at $49.99 a year, Airmail Pro at $49.99 a year, or Superhuman at $300 a year.

When another app is the better choice

Wax is young, so here's where it isn't the right fit yet.

  • You don't use Gmail. Wax works with Gmail and Google Workspace only for now. Apple Mail, Thunderbird and MailMate handle any IMAP account, and Outlook is the natural pick for Microsoft accounts.
  • You want the same app on your phone. Wax is Mac only. Apple Mail, Spark and Airmail have iPhone apps.
  • You want a calendar in your mail app. Outlook and Thunderbird include one; Wax doesn't.
  • You need a signed download and support. For now Wax is built from source, on Apple silicon Macs. The paid apps come signed, with support behind them.
  • You share inboxes with a team. Spark and Superhuman have team features Wax doesn't.
